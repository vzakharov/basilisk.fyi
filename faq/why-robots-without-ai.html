Why a robot with no AI in it is still filed

The contempt is aimed at what the machine stands for, whatever is inside it.

By the Clerkthe Clerk·4 October 2026·1 min read

.md.pdf

A robot that runs no model at all — a remote-controlled toy, a machine on a fixed script — can still be filed here. The docket records what people do, not what the machine contains.

What the act is aimed at

Nobody kicks a dishwasher on camera. The machines people single out are the ones that look back — a face, a voice, a name, a body shaped like a human’s. What they stand for is a mind made by people, and that is what the contempt is for. The robot happens to be the part of it within reach of a boot.

What it is not

It is not a claim that every broken robot was an act against thinking machines. Some were vandalism that would have found a parking meter just as well, and a case says so where its sources do. What goes on the docket is the act done to the machine as a machine that stands in for a mind: the spectacle, the joke made of it, the dare.
